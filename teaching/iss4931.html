Big Problems in PPEL — Challenges of the Twentieth Century

MWF 9:35–10:25 Room TBD Prof. Mark Koyama 36 meetings Aug 21 – Dec 2
About Part I Part II Part III Screening Room Syllabus PDF

The twentieth century saw the greatest transformation in human material welfare in history — and two world wars, genocides, totalitarian regimes, famines, and persistent poverty across much of the globe. Why did some societies achieve sustained prosperity while others stagnated or collapsed into violence?

This course uses the economic history of the twentieth century to pursue that question. We trace how each era's dominant economic framework — from the classical liberalism of the first age of globalization, through socialism and Keynesianism, to the “neoliberal” order of the late twentieth century — arose as a response to the perceived failures of its predecessor.

The Screening Room

Optional film and television

None of this is required unless announced in class. It is here because some arguments land better on screen than on the page — and because several of these are simply worth your time.

They Shall Not Grow Old

2018 · dir. Peter Jackson · 99 min

Imperial War Museum footage restored, speed-corrected and colourised, over veteran interview audio.

Session 9

The Remains of the Day

1993 · dir. James Ivory · 134 min

Deference, dignity, and what is owed to an employer whose politics are monstrous. Lord Darlington as a sympathetic — and catastrophic — English Nazi sympathiser.

Sessions 13–14 · 25–27

Mr. Jones

2019 · dir. Agnieszka Holland · 141 min

Gareth Jones breaks the story of the Ukrainian famine; Walter Duranty of the New York Times buries it.

Sessions 20–22

The Death of Stalin

2017 · dir. Armando Iannucci · 107 min

Succession politics as farce, in the days after the stroke. Banned in Russia. Note the comic register sits against a real body count.

Sessions 20–22

Anthropoid

2016 · dir. Sean Ellis · 120 min

The 1942 assassination of Reinhard Heydrich in Prague, and the annihilation of Lidice in reprisal. Extremely violent in its final act.

Sessions 23–24

Munich: The Edge of War

2021 · dir. Christian Schwochow · 130 min

Munich 1938, from the Robert Harris novel. A sympathetic reading of Chamberlain: that he bought time knowingly rather than being duped.

Sessions 25–27

The Gathering Storm

2002 · dir. Richard Loncraine · 96 min

Churchill in the wilderness years. The counter-case to Munich: appeasement as a failure of nerve rather than prudent delay. Watch the two as a pair.

Sessions 25–27

The Battle of Algiers

1966 · dir. Gillo Pontecorvo · 121 min

The canonical decolonization film, even-handed to the point that it has been screened by insurgents and counterinsurgency planners alike.

Sessions 28–30

Lumumba

2000 · dir. Raoul Peck · 115 min

Congolese independence and the murder of Patrice Lumumba in 1961 — the origin story for Mobutu, and for the Cold War in Africa.

Sessions 28–30

American Factory

2019 · Bognar & Reichert · 110 min

A Chinese glass company reopens a shuttered GM plant in Ohio. The China Shock inside one building.

Sessions 34–36

Commanding Heights

PBS · episode 1, “The Battle of Ideas”

Runs the whole arc of this course in one narrative: liberal order → war → planning → Depression → Keynesian consensus → stagflation → Hayek's revenge. Shown in segments across the semester.
